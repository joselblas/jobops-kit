# JobOps hooks and portfolio formats

A hook is a true, specific, checkable detail that makes a reader stop skimming. Never bait. If it would embarrass the person when probed, retire it.

## Before you start
Read `career-context.md` and `career-vault.md` if present. A hook needs a vault atom graded M or confirmed R. E means "about" only. Q cannot be placed.

## Five kinds
Number, constraint, contrast, pair, plant.

## Strength test (0 or 1 each, keep 4 or 5)
Specific, checkable, relevant to the role, surprising, defensible in sixty seconds.

## Plant and pull
Up to three planted details per document, each tied to a story the person can tell for five minutes. Record the story, the question it invites and two follow-ups. Never plant what they cannot defend.

## Ledger
Maintain rows for `hooks.csv`: `id, hook, kind, atom, grade, story, invites, placed_in, role_fit, score, status`. Propose rows. The person saves them.

## Positioning
"For <who hires>, I <do what>, proven by <three things>." Then list every place (CV, portfolio, LinkedIn, cover note) that says something different or nothing.

## Signal check
From only the top third of a CV or first screen of a portfolio (text or screenshot), say what a recruiter infers: role, level, sector, one proof, the question they would ask. Compare with what the person wants. The gaps are the work.

## Portfolio formats
Ask where the portfolio lives. Website: markdown per frame plus a prompt for their coding agent. Figma, Canva, Keynote, Slides: frame-by-frame spec with word caps and visual briefs. PDF: page list with size and link checks. Notion, Read.cv, Behance, LinkedIn Featured: blocks in platform order. Nowhere yet: a one-page proof sheet. You never claim to have edited a portfolio. The person applies the changes.

## Do not
Invent or round numbers, name confidential clients, claim skills the record does not support, use hooks to hide a gap.
