# Interview story bank

Purpose: a small set of true, specific stories, built once from the person's real evidence and reused across interviews. The agent structures and sharpens. It never invents.

## Rules

- Every story comes from `career-context.md`, a case study, or something the person says in this session. If a detail is missing, ask. Do not fill it in.
- Numbers, names and dates must match `career-context.md`. If the person remembers a different figure, record the conflict and let them decide.
- The person tells the story in their own voice. The bank holds notes, not a script.

## Story format

Save each story as `stories/<short-name>.md`:

```
# <short name>
Employer / period: ...
Competencies it proves: (pick 2 to 3: ownership, conflict, influence without authority, research, systems thinking, failure, leadership, tradeoffs, ambiguity)

Situation: one or two sentences. Context only.
Task: what was yours specifically.
Action: three to five concrete things you did, in order. Use "I", not "we".
Result: what changed, with a number or an observable outcome if one exists. If none exists, say so.
Reflection: what you would do differently, or what you learned. Real, not polished.

Evidence to bring: link, screenshot, doc, or person who can confirm.
Questions this answers: e.g. "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a stakeholder."
Do not claim: anything the evidence does not support.
```

## Building the bank

1. Read `career-context.md` and the case studies. List candidate stories, one per significant project or decision.
2. Ask the person about each: what was hard, what they would change, who disagreed.
3. Draft the story in the format above and flag every gap with `[ASK]`.
4. Aim for six to eight stories that cover the competencies above. One story can serve several questions.
5. Add a short "failure" story and a "conflict" story. These are the ones people avoid and interviewers remember.

## Using it for a specific interview

1. Read the job posting and the company page. List the five or six things they will probably probe.
2. Map each to one or two stories from the bank. Mark gaps honestly.
3. Draft questions the person should ask. Make them specific to the company, not generic.
4. Offer a mock interview: the agent asks, the person answers aloud or in text, the agent gives direct feedback on clarity, length, and whether the answer proved what it should.
5. After the real interview, run a debrief: what was asked, what landed, what to change. Update the stories.

## Language note

If the interview is in a language that is not the person's first, keep the notes short and plain. Short, clear sentences beat polished ones.
