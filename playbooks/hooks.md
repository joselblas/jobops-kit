# Hooks playbook: true details that make people stop

A hook is a true, specific, checkable detail that makes a recruiter stop skimming and start asking. It is not bait. Everything a hook says must be real, sourced in the vault and defensible in sixty seconds. A hook you cannot defend is a liability, not a hook.

## Five kinds

| Kind | What it does | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|
| Number | A measured result, stated plainly | "Cut onboarding from 40 minutes to 12" |
| Constraint | The thing that made it hard | "Shipped with no developer for six weeks" |
| Contrast | Before and after | "From 14 button styles to 3" |
| Pair | Two worlds that rarely meet | "Games degree, now designing clinical software" |
| Plant | A detail placed on purpose so the reader asks about it | "Taught myself to read a heart-rate chart for this project" |

## The strength test

Score each hook 0 or 1 on five questions. Keep hooks scoring 4 or 5.

1. Specific: does it name a thing, a number or a moment, not an adjective?
2. Checkable: could a skeptic verify or probe it?
3. Relevant: does it matter for the role in front of you?
4. Surprising: would a reader not have guessed it?
5. Defensible: can the person tell the full story in sixty seconds?

A hook that is only surprising and not defensible fails. A hook with an E grade may only be used as "about".

## Plant and pull

The strongest use. Place up to three planted details in a CV or portfolio, each tied to a story the person has prepared well. The interviewer asks about what is on the page, so the conversation goes where the person is strongest.

For each plant, record the story it leads to, the question it invites, and the two follow-ups. The person rehearses those three. Never plant a detail they could not talk about for five minutes.

## The hook ledger

`hooks.csv`, in the private folder. Columns: `id, hook, kind, atom, grade, story, invites, placed_in, role_fit, score, status`. See `templates/hooks.example.csv`. Statuses: `idea`, `drafted`, `placed`, `retired`.

Each hook links to the vault atom it came from. If the atom is E or Q, the hook cannot be placed until the person confirms.

## Where hooks live

- CV: the first bullet of each job and the summary line. One hook per bullet at most.
- Portfolio: the outcome strip and the first sentence of each case study.
- Cover note, LinkedIn: the opening line. Lead with a hook, then the ask.
- Interview: the story each plant leads to.

## Positioning: the song you are singing

Before writing hooks for a role, write the positioning in one sentence: "For <who hires>, I <do what>, proven by <three things>." Then check that the CV, portfolio, LinkedIn and cover note all sing that one song. List every place that says something different, or says nothing.

## Signal check: six seconds

Read only the top third of a CV or the first screen of a portfolio. Say what a recruiter would infer: role, level, sector, one proof, and the question they would ask. Compare with what the person wants them to infer. The gaps are the work.

## Rules

- Never invent or round up a number to make a hook land.
- Never name a confidential client, figure or screen. Ask about NDAs.
- No clickbait. If a hook would embarrass the person when probed, retire it.
- A hook serves the person's real strengths. It does not hide a gap, and it does not claim a skill the record does not support.
- Maximum three plants per document.
